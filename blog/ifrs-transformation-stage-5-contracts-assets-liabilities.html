IFRS transformation. Stage 5: contracts, assets, liabilities

1. Contracts

In loan agreements, we look for non-market interest rates, additional fees and special early repayment terms. Depending on what is found, a review of amortised cost, the effective interest rate and the classification of liabilities may be needed.

In lease and leasing agreements, it often turns out that a long-term lease is recorded as current expenses, and there are also extension options and variable payments. Under IFRS 16 this means calculating right-of-use assets and lease liabilities.

Supply contracts may contain prepayments, long payment deferrals and penalties.

In sales contracts, we look at deferred or premature revenue recognition, bonuses, discounts, returns and warranties. We carry out the analysis under IFRS 15, and it is possible that revenue, receivables and contract liabilities will have to be adjusted.

In loan contracts, we come across interest-free financing and loans from owners: here we check the terms, measurement and classification under IFRS 9, as well as the related party disclosures.

2. Assets

In property, plant and equipment, we often find fully depreciated items that are still in use, incorrectly determined useful lives, idle time, and physical or technological obsolescence. Therefore we check depreciation and indicators of impairment.

In intangible assets - incorrect capitalisation of costs, unjustified useful lives and a lack of confirmation of rights to the asset; accordingly, we compare the recognition criteria with IAS 38 and look at amortisation and impairment.

Inventories may turn out to be obsolete, damaged or slow-moving, cost overstated, and the net realisable value analysis absent. We check measurement under IAS 2, together with production costs and the need for a write-down.

We pay special attention to investment property: it happens that items are leased out but accounted for as ordinary property, plant and equipment, or are classified incorrectly. Then it is necessary to determine compliance with the requirements of IAS 40, choose a measurement model and, possibly, recalculate the carrying amount.

3. Liabilities

In liabilities, we are interested in completeness of recognition, and correctness of measurement and classification.

In loans and borrowings, we encounter an incorrect split between current and non-current liabilities, unrecorded fees and market conditions; we analyse them under IFRS 9, determining amortised cost and the effective interest rate.

Liabilities to employees are often not fully accrued - these are payments for leave, bonuses and other benefits, and here we check against IAS 19 and assess the need for additional accruals.

In taxes, we look for errors in the calculation of income tax, unrecorded temporary differences and the absence of deferred taxes; we calculate current and deferred tax under IAS 12. Court cases are existing claims, lawsuits and disputes for which future payments are possible: we assess the need to recognise a provision or disclose contingent liabilities under IAS 37.
