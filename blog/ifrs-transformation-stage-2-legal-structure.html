IFRS transformation. Stage 2: legal structure

Legal structure: why it is the first thing to establish in a transformation

When we start diagnosing a company, the legal structure is not a formality. It is the map of the terrain, without which the rest of the work may turn out to be wasted. Because at this stage we are determining not just "who is connected to whom", but the boundaries of reporting, the scope of the transformation, the need for consolidation and the disclosure risks.

How many companies are actually in the structure?

A business may be economically a single whole, yet legally split into several entities. And the question arises immediately: are we transforming the financial statements of one legal entity, or do we need consolidated financial statements?

Who controls whom - and how to understand it under IFRS

Control under IFRS is not always a formal ownership share. It is important to establish: who the ultimate beneficial owner is, whether there is control through contracts, whether there are nominee owners, whether there is joint control or associates.

What the scope of the transformation depends on

The boundaries of reporting are the scope of work. If there are subsidiaries, additional tasks arise: bringing the financial statements of all companies to uniform accounting policies and a single currency, preparing a consolidation schedule for each of them, eliminating intragroup balances, income, expenses and unrealised profit, determining the non-controlling interest, preparing consolidated notes. The project becomes bigger.

If the company is part of an international group, the task is even harder: we do not create the financial statements "from scratch", but adapt to the group reporting package with its formats, accounting policies, deadline requirements and translation into the parent company's currency.

Related parties - a separate area of attention

Even if consolidation is not required, related parties almost always affect the notes under IAS 24. We need to identify: owners and ultimate beneficial owners, key management personnel, companies under common control, and any companies through which loans, sales, services or leases pass.

Transactions between related parties have specific IFRS consequences. An interest-free loan from an owner - discounting under IFRS 9, possibly a contribution to equity. A lease of a warehouse from the owner's company - a right-of-use asset and a lease liability under IFRS 16. Intragroup sales with a mark-up - in the consolidated financial statements, the internal revenue and unrealised profit must be eliminated if the goods remain in stock.

What to request after the diagnosis of the structure

Corporate documents: ownership structure, extracts from registers, charters, owners' minutes, agreements for the purchase and sale of shares, information on ultimate beneficial owners.

Financial documents: financial statements and trial balance of each company, turnovers and balances, loan schedules, agreements between related parties, reconciliation statements.

Management documents: who makes key decisions, who approves budgets and controls cash flows, who sets prices.
