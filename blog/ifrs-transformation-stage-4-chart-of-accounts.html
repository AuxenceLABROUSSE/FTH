IFRS transformation. Stage 4: chart of accounts

At the diagnostic stage, the analysis of the chart of accounts and the actual accounting under P(S)BO shows how detailed the accounting is and whether the available data is sufficient to prepare IFRS financial statements.

However, the chart of accounts in itself does not show whether the accounting is correct, and the amount of IFRS adjustments cannot be determined from it. Therefore it is necessary to look at three levels. The first level - the chart of accounts itself: how the accounting is structured and which sub-accounts are provided. The second level - the trial balance and accounting registers: which balances and turnovers are actually recorded and whether the necessary analytics exist. The third level - the source documents and the accounting policy, in order to understand the economic substance of the transactions and whether their presentation meets the requirements of IFRS.

In property, plant and equipment (accounts 10, 13, 15), a typical problem is the absence of analytics by object, of separate accounting for modernisations or for construction in progress. In that case, you should obtain the fixed asset register and check depreciation and indicators of impairment. With inventories (20, 22, 23, 26, 28) it is a similar story: when there is a lack of detail by type, batch or production, we analyse separately the cost, impairment and production costs.

For receivables (36, 37, 38) without a breakdown by repayment terms and types, we prepare an ageing analysis of overdue amounts and expected credit losses, and at the same time work out which of these are really financial assets and which are not. With loans and borrowings (50, 60 and others) it is the same, only the breakdown is needed by terms, currency, lenders and contracts; then we check classification, discounting and the effective interest rate. Separately, it is worth looking at the settlements on accounts 63 and 68, because if heterogeneous liabilities are recorded there, they need to be analysed and reclassified by economic substance.

Income (70, 71, 73 and others) is often not split by types of contracts and performance obligations to customers; in that case it is worth analysing the contracts under IFRS 15. In expenses (23, 91-97), the economic nature is sometimes not visible, and some of it may have been mistakenly attributed to cost of sales, so it is worth checking classification, capitalisation and the correctness of period allocation. Provisions (47) are often missing for individual types of obligations, so it is worth looking at leave, warranties, court cases, bonuses. In taxes (64, 98 and others), there is often no separate accounting for deferred taxes, so it is necessary to obtain the tax bases and prepare calculations under IAS 12.

Now - related parties. If transactions with group companies and owners cannot be identified, it is necessary to draw up a register of related parties and additionally analyse them under IFRS 9 and IAS 24. And if the adjustments of prior years (44 and others) contain corrections and unclear manual entries, this means that their nature must be established and their effect on the IFRS opening statement of financial position assessed.

However, if there is no separate sub-account, this does not mean that the accounting is kept incorrectly, since the necessary information may be in analytical registers, ERP modules or supporting calculations.
