How to start an IFRS financial reporting transformation: the first stage

Before moving on to figures and adjustments, one basic question has to be answered: why does the company need this?

The business goal of the transformation determines the level of detail, the budget, the timeline, the need for auditors and the depth of estimates. Meeting a legal requirement is one thing. Preparing to attract an investor, sell the business or enter international markets is something else entirely. So the first step is to state the goal clearly and decide straight away: is this a one-off transformation or a move to permanent IFRS accounting? This matters, because the logic of the whole project depends on it.

Diagnostics: what needs to be analysed

Once the goal is set, the company goes through diagnostics. At this stage the current state of accounting, processes and data is assessed to understand the scale of the changes ahead. The transformation specialist examines:

  • The legal structure - a single legal entity, a group or related parties.
  • The financial statements - the statement of financial position, profit or loss, cash flows, changes in equity and the notes.
  • The accounting system - BAS/1C, an ERP or Excel.
  • Assets - property, plant and equipment, intangible assets, inventories, investment property.
  • Liabilities - loans, provisions, taxes, litigation.
  • Contracts - leases, leasing, supply, loans (this matters for IFRS 16 and IFRS 9).
  • Income and expenses - customer contracts, bonuses, discounts, prepayments, capitalisation and provisioning issues.
  • Deferred taxes - deferred tax assets and liabilities for income tax.
  • Related parties - owners, management, group transactions.
  • The functional currency and exchange differences.
  • The auditor's expectations - if an audit is planned, it has a significant effect on disclosure requirements.

As each of these areas has its own specifics and nuances, it makes sense to cover them in the next posts.

The diagnostic report

The diagnostics result in a document that records the main differences between P(S)BO and IFRS, what needs to be recalculated, which data is missing and which risks were identified. In effect, this report is the roadmap for all the work that follows.

Setting the transition dates

Right after the diagnostics, the timeline has to be set in line with IFRS 1:

  • The date of transition to IFRS - the beginning of the earliest comparative period.
  • The first IFRS reporting period.
  • The comparative period.
  • The opening IFRS statement of financial position at the date of transition.

Without clearly defined dates, correct comparative financial statements cannot be prepared.
